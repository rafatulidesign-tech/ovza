A living structure

A Trust That Continues to Work

A trust is not simply a document you create and store away. It’s a living framework that continues working for decades, responding to the life events and circumstances of the people it’s designed to support.

When you establish a trust today, you create a structure that can adapt as your family grows, as new generations are born, and as life circumstances change. The trustee administers the trust over time, honouring your original intentions while responding to real-world needs.

The trustee does not simply hold the documents. The trustee continues administering the structure as life unfolds.

One trust. Many possibilities.

What Can You Do With a Trust?

A single structure can support the people and purposes that matter most to you — today and for years to come.

Fund Education

Provide for school, university or professional training through regular payments, occasional distributions or direct payment of fees — while the capital remains protected.

Support Family Members

A structured path to support loved ones — regular income, distributions tied to life events, or funds for housing, healthcare and emergencies.

Preserve Wealth for Generations

Keep assets within the family structure rather than dividing them at once, providing continuity for succession planning across generations.

Manage Investments & Assets

Hold portfolios, business shares or real estate, managed by the trustee according to your framework while you retain the governance you designed.

Provide Healthcare & Care

Ensure funds are available for medical treatment, long-term care or nursing — a dedicated provision kept separate from general distributions.

Ensure Business Continuity

Hold company shares and set a clear succession path, helping prevent ownership disputes and keeping the business running according to your intentions.

The essentials

How a Trust Works

  • The Settlor

    You — the person who creates the trust and sets its framework.

  • The Trustee

    Accepts responsibility for holding and administering the trust’s assets.

  • The Trust Deed

    The binding legal document that defines the structure and its rules.

  • The Beneficiaries

    The people who receive support as defined in the deed.

  • The Letter of Wishes

    Your personal guidance for the trustee’s decisions.

Two documents, one structure

Understanding the Structure

The Trust Deed

Your legal framework

  • Binding legal document that establishes the trust
  • Names the trustee and sets their powers
  • Identifies the beneficiaries
  • Sets rules for managing and distributing assets
  • Outlines amendment and termination provisions
+

The Letter of Wishes

Your personal guidance

  • Supplements the deed with your intentions
  • Provides context for the trustee’s decisions
  • Non-binding, flexible and personal
  • Can address special circumstances or needs
  • Updated more easily than the deed

Together, they create your complete trust structure — a legal framework backed by your personal vision.

What Can a Trust Hold?

A trust can hold a wide variety of assets, either directly or through an underlying company structure.

  • Liquid assetsCash and bank deposits
  • InvestmentsPortfolios, securities, bonds and funds
  • Real estateProperty interests and land
  • Business interestsShares in family or operating companies
  • Intellectual propertyPatents, trademarks and copyrights
  • InsuranceLife insurance policies
  • Alternative assetsArtwork, collectibles and precious metals
  • Holding companyAn underlying company the trust owns
